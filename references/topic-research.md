# Topic research

Research a topic to teach it, not to collect links.

## Evidence plan

1. Turn the learning objective into three to six research questions.
2. Search current sources. For technical questions, rely on papers and official
   documentation. For finance, law, medicine, policy, or other high-stakes
   domains, prefer regulators, public institutions, standards bodies, primary
   research, and authoritative reference material.
3. Gather enough sources to triangulate the mechanism, important numbers,
   limitations, and disagreement. Three to eight strong sources are usually
   better than a long undifferentiated bibliography.
4. Open and read the passages that support planned claims. A search snippet or
   citation title is not evidence.
5. Record publication date, event or data date, and jurisdiction where they
   affect meaning.

## Research ledger

Maintain a working ledger with:

- Planned claim or relationship.
- Source and supporting passage or data.
- Confidence and freshness.
- Whether the page presents it as fact, model, assumption, or simplification.
- Candidate interaction, if the relationship changes under learner input.

Prefer relationships that can be observed or calculated. Do not force
qualitative background material into sliders or scores.

## Domain boundaries

- Keep financial explainers educational. Do not turn a conceptual request into
  personalized investment advice or invent live market inputs.
- Keep medical and legal explainers informational and jurisdiction-aware.
- Separate stable mechanisms from current rates, laws, product behavior, or
  benchmarks that may change.
- Surface genuine disagreement instead of collapsing it into false certainty.

## Completion condition

Research is sufficient when the spine and every planned interaction are backed
by evidence, important caveats are known, and no load-bearing claim depends on
memory alone.
